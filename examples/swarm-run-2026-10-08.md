# Swarm and core-loop runs, 2026-10-08

The record of the live runs the README's "Many agents at once" numbers come from. Every number here was measured on the `dev-oscabriel` stage on 2026-10-08 (UTC), on the Workers deployed from commit `c7b68ff` (every Answer instance in the runs carries the same Worker version). The runs started from commits `25a727e` and `6730a0e`, which change only the swarm's own scripts. Nothing here is rounded or estimated; a number that missed its bar says so.

## The swarm

Each run seeded a fresh copy of burrow, the fictional six-service example, with `bun run seed -- --stage dev-oscabriel --as burrow-swarm-<n>`, then ran `bun run swarm -- --stage dev-oscabriel --project burrow-swarm-<n> --scenario video` headless. Six fictional contributors on four harnesses each claimed one child of burrow's `drop-legacy-fetch` family (a migrate ask split into one child per module that calls `legacyFetch`, plus a delete child that waits on them all) and answered it at the same time. Time is from the contributor's claim to the end of its run. Refusals counts what the contributor's `status` shows: a push refused in git's own output is not an answer there, so it is not counted.

- **ivo**: its first answer was refused `ChecksFailed` by burrow's lint lane. It fixed the answer and pushed again in the same session, and that answer landed.

It then submitted the rebased head with `submit --base <new main>`, and that answer landed as `13ef502`. In a family, the rebase that git suggests after `StaleBase` was refused, and only the contract's `--base` path worked.
- Every landing but tamsin's carries an `Assisted-by` trailer naming its harness; OpenCode's has none.

## The core loop

Five runs of `bun scripts/smoke.ts --stage dev-oscabriel --loop`, at commit `6730a0e`, one after another from 16:48:58 to 16:59 UTC. All five passed. Each run makes a throwaway project, opens an ask red first, claims it, pushes outside the ask's territory and is refused `OutOfScope` in git's output, then pushes the fixed answer, waits for it to land and reads its verdict note. The `landing` step is the whole asynchronous verdict, from the push taken to main moved. The Answer workflow's merge, checks and land steps for each loop landing, in seconds: 11/3/6, 14/3/7, 12/2/6, -/3/9 and 7/4/4; the land step's median is 6 s and its worst 9 s. In the run that took 91.9 s, the push was taken at 16:55:09 and its Answer workflow began 66 s later, at the Project's alarm; the platform's logs for that window hold nothing above info level, and the cause was not traced.

The ask's check, red twice on main in `open-ask`, took 21.6 to 23.5 s, under the 60 s bar for a small check; the loop's `checks` step took 2 to 4 s.
